# INSURANCE-RISK-CLAIMS-ANALYSIS
This analysis explores insurance claims data to uncover which factors drive claim amounts and which segments carry the most risk. Private vehicles dominate claims, middle-aged drivers lead in volume, and coverage zones are evenly distributed.

## Conclusion

Insurance risk is not evenly distributed. Private vehicles, middle-aged drivers, and certain car makes contribute disproportionately to claims. Coverage zones are balanced, and newer cars show higher claim amounts up to a point. Education and marital status also matter, with single policyholders and Bachelor's degree holders representing significant claim volumes. These insights can help insurers refine pricing, target risk, and improve claims management.

## Recommendation

Based on the insights uncovered, the following recommendations are proposed to improve risk assessment and claims management.

- Strengthen risk assessment for private vehicle policyholders and middle-aged drivers (26-55), as these segments account for the majority of claim amounts. Consider adjusting premiums or introducing targeted risk mitigation programs for these groups.

- Investigate the high claim amounts associated with Ford and Chevrolet vehicles. Understanding the root causes, whether repair costs, driver behavior, or vehicle safety, could inform pricing and underwriting decisions.

- Recognize that coverage zones are evenly distributed. This suggests that geographic location alone is not a strong predictor of claims. Instead, focus on other factors such as car use, age, and marital status.

- Consider the impact of car year on claims. Newer vehicles are associated with higher claim amounts, potentially due to higher repair costs. Review pricing and coverage options for newer vehicles to ensure profitability.

- Use education and marital status as part of a broader risk segmentation strategy. Single policyholders and those with certain education levels represent higher claim volumes. Tailored offerings, such as safe driver discounts or loyalty programs, could help manage risk while retaining valuable customers.

-  Continue monitoring claims by kids driving. The sharp drop in claims from age 0 to age 3 suggests that younger drivers are a lower-risk segment. Consider offering competitive rates to attract this demographic.
